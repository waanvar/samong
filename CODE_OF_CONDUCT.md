# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Criticise code, not the person who wrote it. Assume the
other person is trying.

## What this covers

Issues, pull requests, commit messages, code review, and any other space that
belongs to this project. It applies to everyone equally, including the
maintainer.

## What is expected

- **Argue about the work.** "This breaks on a case-insensitive filesystem" is
  useful. "You clearly don't know how filesystems work" is not, and it is also
  a worse argument, because it stops the reader from checking whether you are
  right.
- **Take a correction as information.** Being wrong in public is a normal part
  of building something; a fair number of this project's own mistakes are
  written down in `docs/RELEASE-LESSONS.md` for that reason.
- **Accept a "no" about scope.** `CONTRIBUTING.md` lists changes that will be
  declined and why. Disagreeing with a constraint is fine; relitigating it in
  every thread is not.
- **Remember that people here have different first languages.** This project's
  commit history is largely Thai and its documentation is English. Read past
  awkward phrasing rather than scoring points off it.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment, insults, or sustained hostility. Comments demeaning someone for who
they are — including race, nationality, gender, gender identity, sexual
orientation, disability, age, religion, or level of experience. Sexual attention
or imagery. Publishing someone's private information. Deliberately derailing
threads or wearing people down until they leave.

Being technically correct does not license any of the above.

## Reporting

Email **waanvar@gmail.com** with `samong conduct` in the subject. Say what
happened and link to it if it is public.

Please be realistic about what happens next: **this is a one-person project.**
There is no committee and no appeals process. You will get a reply, the report
will be kept private, and I will act on it — which in practice means editing or
deleting comments, closing a thread, or blocking an account from the repository.
I am also the person most likely to be the subject of a report, which is worth
saying out loud rather than pretending otherwise. If that makes you
uncomfortable reporting to me, GitHub's own abuse reporting exists and is
independent of this project.
